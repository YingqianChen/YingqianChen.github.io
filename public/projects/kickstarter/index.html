Kickstarter Campaign Performance Analysis

Funding goals, backer support and regional differences across 197,675 projects — with a detailed examination of Hong Kong.

Web Robots Kickstarter · September 2025 snapshot · Projects launched between April 2009 and September 2025

Research framework

What is associated with campaign success?

Kickstarter campaigns differ in their funding targets, audiences and market context. This analysis examines how these differences relate to observed outcomes, then tests whether Hong Kong’s high success rate remains when comparable campaign groups are examined. The findings describe this dataset; they do not establish what causes a campaign to succeed.

02 · Campaign characteristics

Higher funding targets are associated with lower success rates.

A funding goal describes the target set by a creator, whereas backer count records support received during the campaign. Campaign duration adds a third dimension. These variables should be interpreted separately: observed backer support is an outcome-related measure, not information available before launch.

Funding goals: campaign volume and success rate

The left panel shows how many completed campaigns fall in each USD goal band. The right panel shows their success rate, in the same row order.

Success rates fall from approximately 76% in the lowest goal band to 13% in the highest. This is a descriptive association, not a recommendation to lower every funding target: project scope and category also differ across these bands. The volume panel makes the number of campaigns behind each rate visible.

Campaign duration and success

Use the category filter to check whether the overall duration pattern also appears within a specific type of project.

Duration is calculated from the exact time between launch and deadline. For example, “>14–30” includes campaigns lasting more than 14 and up to 30 days. Category-specific views require at least 20 completed campaigns per band. Differences between bands do not establish that changing duration would improve success.

Backer support: concentration and pledged funding

Read campaign counts alongside the median pledged amount for the same backer bands. The median is the middle campaign value, so it is less affected by unusually large campaigns than an average.

Across all project states, 77.0% of projects have fewer than 100 backers. The completed-campaign comparison above shows how campaign frequency and typical funding differ across support levels. Backer count and funding are jointly observed outcomes; this relationship should not be presented as a pre-launch prediction.

03 · Categories, recognition and time

Project volume, success and funding describe different aspects of performance.

A category can attract many campaigns without having the highest success rate. Similarly, a large funding total does not describe the typical project. The following comparisons distinguish participation, outcomes and financial scale rather than combining them into a single ranking.

Category profile: scale alongside success

By default, the 12 largest eligible categories appear with both project count and success rate. Changing the analytical focus updates the ranking and left-hand measure; success rate remains visible on the right.

Tabletop Games has the largest project count in this sample: 6,633 projects, with a 92.7% success rate among its 6,103 completed campaigns. Other rankings answer different questions. For example, total pledged emphasizes aggregate financial scale, while median pledged describes a more typical campaign.

How pledged funding is distributed across categories

Tile area represents total pledged funding in USD across all project states. The 12 largest categories are shown individually; the rest are combined. Color separates tiles and does not encode success.

The treemap provides a part-to-whole overview. For precise comparison between categories, use the aligned chart above or the data table below; area is less suitable than position or length for reading small differences.

Staff Pick: recognition and observed performance

Success rate and typical funding are shown together for campaigns with and without the Staff Pick flag.

Staff Pick campaigns have a completed success rate of approximately 92.2%, compared with 56.4% for other campaigns. However, selection is not random: featured projects may already differ in quality, visibility or momentum. These data support an association, not an estimate of the effect of receiving a Staff Pick.

Spotlight: why the timing of a variable matters

The Spotlight flag coincides with successful status in this snapshot. Its apparent relationship with success therefore cannot be treated as an independent predictive finding.

A model intended to guide creators before launch must not use information that becomes available only after the outcome. Spotlight illustrates this risk, often called outcome leakage. The financial comparison is descriptive and does not change that limitation.

Annual funding: total scale and the typical campaign

The upper chart shows the sum across each launch-year cohort; the lower chart shows the median project in that same cohort. Both charts share the year order but use independent USD scales.

Median per campaign

Total funding reflects both the number of projects and their funding amounts. The median helps assess whether an unusually high total also corresponds to a higher typical value. These are launch-year groups in a snapshot, not a record of payments received during each calendar year. Pledged amounts represent commitments and should not be interpreted as collected revenue, particularly for failed, canceled or live campaigns. The 2025 group ends on September 11 and must not be compared with full years as an annual growth measure.

04 · Geographic comparison

The largest country sample is not necessarily the most successful.

Country counts describe representation in the dataset; success rates describe outcomes among completed campaigns. Showing them together reveals whether a high rate is supported by a large or relatively small sample. Differences may also reflect category mix, funding goals and the periods covered.

05 · Hong Kong in depth

Hong Kong’s higher success rate requires a like-for-like comparison.

Among completed campaigns, Hong Kong has an 84.6% success rate, compared with 61.7% outside Hong Kong — a difference of 22.9 percentage points. Before interpreting this as a regional advantage, the analysis examines which projects make up the local sample and whether the difference persists within comparable categories and funding-goal bands.

Hong Kong’s category profile

Gadgets, Product Design and Hardware contribute 543, 534 and 358 projects respectively to the full Hong Kong sample of 2,978 projects. The comparison below displays eligible categories with at least 30 completed campaigns.

This concentration matters because categories have different baseline success rates. A region with more campaigns in high-success categories can have a higher overall rate even before any within-category difference is considered.

Within-category success rates: Hong Kong and elsewhere

Each row compares the same category. Amber circles show Hong Kong; blue diamonds show all other countries combined. The connecting line shows the difference on one shared percentage scale.

Only categories with at least 30 completed Hong Kong campaigns and 100 elsewhere are included. This comparison reduces category-level differences in composition, but it does not yet account for funding goals within each category. Sample sizes and percentage-point differences are available for every row.

From the full sample to a composition-adjusted benchmark

Read the first two rows as one comparison and the last two as a second comparison. The second pair is restricted to the same eligible category × goal-band groups, rather than the full sample.

How the adjusted comparison is constructed

  1. Divide completed projects into groups defined by category and USD funding-goal band.
  2. Retain groups with at least five Hong Kong campaigns and 20 campaigns elsewhere. The resulting 80 groups contain 2,332 Hong Kong campaigns, or 84.3% of its 2,765 completed campaigns.
  3. Calculate the outside-Hong-Kong success rate within each group, then weight those rates by the distribution of Hong Kong campaigns across the retained groups.
  4. Compare this weighted outside rate with Hong Kong’s rate within those same groups.

On this comparable subset, Hong Kong’s success rate is 90.4%, versus an 81.3% weighted outside benchmark: a remaining difference of 9.1 percentage points. The comparison shows that the unadjusted 22.9-point difference is sensitive to the population and project mix being compared. Because both weighting and sample restriction change, the reduction cannot be attributed to category and goal composition alone.

Conclusion: Hong Kong retains a higher observed success rate within the groups covered by this analysis. This is not evidence that locating a campaign in Hong Kong causes better outcomes. Launch period, creator experience, platform exposure and project quality are not controlled, and the adjusted result does not cover all Hong Kong campaigns.
Sensitivity check · Japan

A single large funding goal can dominate an annual total.

In both 2023 and 2024, Japan’s sample includes a campaign with a 1-billion-JPY funding goal. Each accounts for more than 70% of its year’s native-currency goal total, while the median goal in both years is 300,000 JPY. This is why a total alone is insufficient evidence of a broad increase in campaign scale.

Japan: total and median funding goals

Median funding goal per campaign

The median provides a more stable description of the typical funding target when extreme goals dominate a total. The 2025 cohort is incomplete and is included for context, not for a full-year growth comparison.

Methodology and visual design

Consistent definitions make the comparisons interpretable.

The standardized table contains 197,676 cleaned records. Removing one duplicated project ID leaves 197,675 unique projects. Funding goals are converted to USD using the source static exchange rates. Counts and funding summaries include all project states unless a chart specifies completed campaigns; success rates always use successful ÷ (successful + failed).

Why aligned panels, not dual axes?

Counts and percentages need to be read together, but their scales are unrelated. Aligned panels retain the same categories while labeling each measure independently. This avoids suggesting a relationship through the arbitrary alignment of two overlaid axes. When the unit is identical, as in the Hong Kong rate comparison, both series share one scale.

Where interaction adds value

The principal comparisons are visible without switching metrics. Controls are reserved for changing categories, minimum sample sizes, ranking criteria or funding definitions. Direct mark inspection and data tables make the chart values accessible to readers who need more detail.

Analysis and delivery

R and Tableau supported the original distribution analysis and exploratory visualizations. The web report uses audited aggregate summaries recomputed in Python and interactive SVG charts, with keyboard access and downloadable tables. No row-level project extract is sent to the browser.

Limits of the evidence

This is a descriptive analysis of the available snapshot, not a complete census of Kickstarter activity or a causal study. Currency conversion, unequal country coverage, incomplete recent cohorts and unobserved project characteristics limit the conclusions. Observed relationships should guide further investigation, not be interpreted as guaranteed success strategies.

Source: Web Robots Kickstarter September 2025 snapshot. Launch dates in the analyzed sample: April 25, 2009–September 11, 2025. This is an independent portfolio project, not an official Kickstarter performance report.
