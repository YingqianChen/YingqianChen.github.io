## Problem

Bike-sharing systems fail operationally when stations run empty during the
morning rush or overflow at night. Rebalancing fleets across the city is
expensive, and its cost scales with forecast error — a 10% improvement in
demand prediction translates directly into fewer unnecessary truck movements.

This project builds an end-to-end pipeline to forecast hourly rental counts
for Washington D.C.'s Capital Bikeshare system (2011–2012) and benchmarks four
models to answer two questions: **which model family best captures the
temporal structure of bike demand, and which features matter most?**

## Dataset

The data is the Kaggle "Bike Sharing Demand" dataset. Each row is one hour of
system-wide rentals. The training set contains the first 19 days of each
month, the test set the remainder.

| Field | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `datetime` | timestamp | hourly timestamp (parsed, chronologically sorted) |
| `season` | categorical (1–4) | spring / summer / fall / winter |
| `holiday` | binary | whether the day is a holiday |
| `workingday` | binary | weekday and not a holiday |
| `weather` | categorical (1–4) | clear → mist/cloudy → light rain/snow → heavy rain/snow |
| `temp` | numeric (°C) | actual temperature |
| `atemp` | numeric (°C) | "feels like" temperature |
| `humidity` | numeric (%) | relative humidity |
| `windspeed` | numeric (km/h) | wind speed |
| `casual` | numeric | casual-user rentals (train only) |
| `registered` | numeric | registered-user rentals (train only) |
| `count` | numeric | total rentals — **target** = casual + registered (train only) |

- **Training set**: 10,886 records × 12 columns.
- **Test set**: 6,493 records × 9 columns (no `casual` / `registered` / `count`).

## Exploratory data analysis

The target is strongly right-skewed, so the distribution is examined first.

| Metric | Raw `count` | `log1p(count)` |
|---|---|---|
| Skewness | 1.242 | −0.851 |
| Kurtosis | 1.299 | −0.120 |
| Mean | 191.57 | 4.591 |
| Std | 181.14 | 1.419 |
| Median | 145.00 | 4.984 |
| Max | 977 | 6.886 |

`log1p` cuts the absolute skewness by 31.5% (1.242 → −0.851) and brings the
excess kurtosis near zero (−0.120). This is why **every model** trains on
`log1p(count)` and back-transforms predictions with `expm1`; the two neural
models additionally standardize the log target.

## Data cleaning

There are **no `NaN` values**, but `windspeed == 0` is implausible and treated
as missing: **1,313 records in train** and **867 in test**. A Random Forest
regressor (fit on the non-zero wind records using season, temperature,
humidity, hour and weekday as predictors) imputes these values. The imputer is
fit on training data only and then applied to both splits, keeping the
train/test boundary leakage-free.

## Feature engineering

Three groups expand the 9 raw columns into **40 features** (6 categorical, 34
numeric):

1. **Calendar & cyclical** — year/month/hour/weekday plus sine/cosine encodings
   of hour, weekday and month; weekend/peak/commute flags; a `days_since_2011`
   growth trend.
2. **Weather interactions** — `temp_diff`, `humidity_wind`, `feels_hot`,
   `feels_cold`.
3. **Temporal target-encoded (TTE)** — mean/median of `log1p(count)` grouped by
   five calendar keys (10 columns):

| TTE group | Grouping key |
|---|---|
| `tte_hour_wd` | (hour, workingday) |
| `tte_hour_weekday` | (hour, weekday) |
| `tte_hour_season` | (hour, season) |
| `tte_hour_month` | (hour, month) |
| `tte_year_month` | (year, month) |

TTE aggregates are computed **only from training targets** and looked up by
calendar keys available in both splits — no leakage across the Kaggle boundary.

## Modeling & hyperparameter tuning

| Model | Role |
|---|---|
| Linear Regression | Interpretable baseline (standardized numeric + one-hot categorical) |
| Random Forest | RandomizedSearchCV over 6 candidates × 3 time-ordered folds (18 fits); best: `n_estimators=150`, `max_depth=12`, `min_samples_split=2`, `min_samples_leaf=2`, `max_features=sqrt` |
| ANN (PyTorch MLP) | 6 architectures searched; best `(128, 64, 32)` with dropout 0.2, lr 5e-4, batch 256, weight decay 1e-4 |
| **BiLSTM** | bidirectional, 2 layers; gradient clipping, LayerNorm, weight decay, ReduceLROnPlateau, early stopping (attention ablated — see below) |

Two evaluation scales are used and should not be conflated: **hyperparameter
search** reports MSE on the *standardized log target* over an inner
time-ordered split, while **final results** report RMSE/MAE/R²/MAPE on the
*raw count* over the outer holdout.

The BiLSTM search (6 configurations) ranks the candidates by validation MSE:

| seq_len | hidden | layers | dropout | attention | val loss (MSE) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **48** | **64** | **2** | **0.3** | **No** | **0.064** |
| 48 | 64 | 2 | 0.4 | Yes | 0.108 |
| 24 | 64 | 2 | 0.3 | Yes | 0.111 |
| 24 | 128 | 2 | 0.4 | Yes | 0.115 |
| 48 | 128 | 2 | 0.3 | Yes | 0.123 |
| 24 | 64 | 1 | 0.3 | Yes | 0.155 |

The most instructive result is an **ablation, not an architecture win**: with
48-hour windows and rich TTE features already encoding typical demand, enabling
learned attention pooling made generalization *worse* — the best configuration
uses `use_attention=False` and beats the best attention-enabled variant
(0.064 vs. 0.108).

## Training & validation strategy

All splits are **chronological** to prevent look-ahead leakage:

- **Outer holdout**: the last 20% of the training set — 8,708 records
  (2011-01-01 → 2012-08-05) for training, 2,178 records (2012-08-05 →
  2012-12-19) for validation.
- **Random Forest**: `TimeSeriesSplit` (3 folds) for cross-validated tuning.
- **Neural models**: a time-ordered inner split drives early stopping with
  best-state restoration during tuning (BiLSTM patience 12); the winner is then
  retrained on the full training block.

## Results

| Model | RMSE ↓ | MAE ↓ | R² ↑ | MAPE ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **BiLSTM** | **85.03** | **54.42** | **0.847** | **25.8%** |
| ANN | 100.28 | 67.05 | 0.788 | 27.8% |
| Random Forest | 105.25 | 72.09 | 0.766 | 29.7% |
| Linear Regression | 105.94 | 70.59 | 0.763 | 29.3% |

The BiLSTM reduces RMSE by **19.7%** against the linear baseline
(105.94 → 85.03) and **15.2%** against the ANN. The clearest evidence is
temporal: all four models reproduce the daily cycle, but the BiLSTM and Random
Forest track the sharp commute peaks most closely, while the ANN
systematically under-predicts them. (Random Forest looks competitive only on
this short window — across the full holdout it trails the BiLSTM by ~20 RMSE
points, as the table shows.)

## Error analysis

Segmenting the 2,178 validation records by behavioral regime shows where the
remaining error lives:

| Segment | n | share | mean abs. error | max abs. error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Working-day peak (7–9, 17–19h) | 366 | 16.8% | **117.9** | **618.9** |
| Weekend / holiday | 715 | 32.8% | 47.4 | 302.8 |
| Working-day off-peak | 1,097 | 50.4% | 37.8 | 333.0 |

Three points stand out:

1. **Peak hours are disproportionately hard.** They make up only 16.8% of
   records but carry **3.1× the mean absolute error** of off-peak hours — the
   commute windows are both the highest-demand and the hardest-to-predict
   periods.
2. **Average and worst-case error do not always move together.** Off-peak
   hours have the lowest mean error yet still reach a maximum error of 333.0,
   above the weekend's 302.8 — occasional outliers persist even in the
   "easiest" regime.
3. **The practical implication is targeting.** Rebalancing effort is most
   valuable at commute peaks, where forecast error — and therefore the cost of
   a wrong dispatch — is largest.

The diagnostic below confirms this: predictions hug the ideal `y = x` line
overall, while the residual heatmap concentrates error in weekday commute
hours.

## Interpretability (SHAP)

SHAP TreeExplainer on a 500-observation validation sample quantifies feature
attribution. The TTE features account for **63.2% of total |SHAP|** and occupy
**10 of the top 25** positions — with `days_since_2011` (the growth trend) the
highest-ranked non-TTE feature.

## Conclusion & limitations

The best model (a regularized BiLSTM) reduces RMSE by 19.7% vs. the linear
baseline, and the pipeline's two strongest contributions are concrete: the
leakage-free TTE features (63.2% of SHAP attribution) and the architectural
upgrade that resolved overfitting. The trained model generates the hourly
test-set submission used for Kaggle evaluation.

Remaining limitations and next steps:

- **Point predictions only** — no uncertainty estimates; peak-hour
  under-prediction remains the dominant residual.
- **Peak-hour specialization** — a dedicated quantile model for commute windows
  could target the 3.1× error directly.
- **Richer inputs** — station-level spatial features, lagged demand, and
  calendar events are the natural next additions.

The full pipeline, hyperparameter search and outputs are in the repository's
`final/code/` directory.
