## Problem

Bike-sharing systems fail operationally when stations run empty during the
morning rush or overflow at night. Rebalancing fleets across the city is
expensive, and its cost scales with forecast error — a 10% improvement in
demand prediction translates directly into fewer unnecessary truck movements
and higher user satisfaction.

This project forecasts hourly rental counts for Washington D.C.'s Capital
Bikeshare system (2011–2012, the Kaggle "Bike Sharing Demand" dataset) and
benchmarks four models of increasing complexity to answer two questions:
**which model family best captures the temporal structure of bike demand, and
which features matter most?**

## Data & preprocessing

- **Training set**: 10,886 hourly records (days 1–19 of each month);
  **test set**: 6,493 records (days 20 onward).
- **Target**: total `count`, strongly right-skewed (skewness 1.24) → transformed
  with `log1p`; the neural models additionally standardized the log-target.
- **Missing windspeed**: 1,313 zero-valued records were treated as missing and
  imputed with a Random Forest regressor fit only on training data, avoiding
  leakage.

The target transform is the single most consequential preprocessing choice.
`log1p` turns the right-skewed count into a near-symmetric variable, and the
Q-Q plot confirms the log-transformed target is close to normal — which is why
all neural models were trained on it and predictions back-transformed with
`expm1`.

## Feature engineering

Three groups of features are built inside a scikit-learn pipeline:

1. **Calendar & cyclical** — year/month/hour/weekday plus sine/cosine encodings
   of hour, weekday and month, weekend/peak/commute flags, and a
   `days_since_2011` growth trend.
2. **Weather interactions** — `temp_diff` (felt vs. actual temperature),
   `humidity_wind`, `feels_hot` and `feels_cold`.
3. **Temporal target-encoded (TTE)** — mean/median of `log1p(count)` grouped by
   `(hour, workingday)`, `(hour, weekday)`, `(hour, season)`, `(hour, month)`
   and `(year, month)`, computed exclusively from training-set targets to keep
   the Kaggle train/test boundary leakage-free.

## Models

| Model | Role |
|---|---|
| Linear Regression | Interpretable baseline (standardized numeric + one-hot categorical) |
| Random Forest | Tuned via randomized search over time-ordered cross-validation folds |
| ANN (PyTorch MLP) | `(128, 64, 32)` hidden units, dropout 0.2, weight decay |
| **BiLSTM + attention** | `seq_len=48`, `hidden=64`, 2 layers, bidirectional; gradient clipping, LayerNorm, weight decay, ReduceLROnPlateau, early stopping |

The most instructive result is an **ablation, not an architecture win**: with
48-hour windows and rich TTE features already encoding typical demand, enabling
learned attention pooling made generalization *worse* — the best configuration
used `use_attention=False` (validation loss 0.064 vs. 0.108 for the best
attention variant).

## Results

| Model | RMSE ↓ | MAE ↓ | R² ↑ | MAPE ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **BiLSTM** | **85.03** | **54.42** | **0.847** | **25.8%** |
| ANN | 100.28 | 67.05 | 0.788 | 27.8% |
| Random Forest | 105.25 | 72.09 | 0.766 | 29.7% |
| Linear Regression | 105.94 | 70.59 | 0.763 | 29.3% |

The BiLSTM reduces RMSE by 19.3% against the linear baseline (105.94 → 85.03)
and 15.2% against the ANN. Point metrics aside, the clearest evidence is
temporal: all four models reproduce the daily cycle, but the BiLSTM and Random
Forest track the sharp commute peaks most closely, while the ANN
systematically under-predicts them.

  <figcaption>Validation predictions (first 168 hours) — all four models overlaid on actual demand.</figcaption>

Regularization mattered: the training and validation loss curves stay tightly
coupled until early stopping triggers around epoch 38, confirming the
overfitting seen in the original unregularized LSTM was resolved.

Error analysis by behavioral regime shows working-day peak hours (7–9 and
17–19) carry **3.1× the mean absolute error** of off-peak hours — the commute
windows are both the highest-demand and the hardest-to-predict periods. The
two-panel diagnostic below confirms this: predictions hug the ideal `y = x`
line overall, while the residual heatmap concentrates the remaining error in
weekday commute hours.

## Interpretability (SHAP)

SHAP TreeExplainer on a 500-observation validation sample ranks the top four
features by mean |SHAP| as **all TTE variables**, with the `days_since_2011`
growth trend sixth — validating the feature-engineering design rather than
leaving it as an assumption.

The beeswarm adds directional detail: high TTE values (red) push predictions
up, while high humidity and severe weather (`cat_weather_3`) push them down.

## Outcome & limitations

- BiLSTM reduces RMSE 19.3% vs. the linear baseline and 15.2% vs. the ANN.
- Point predictions only — no uncertainty estimates, and peak-hour
  under-prediction remains the dominant residual.
- Next steps: a dedicated quantile model for peak windows, encoder-decoder
  multi-step forecasting, and station-level spatial features.

The full pipeline, hyperparameter search and outputs are in the repository's
`final/code/` directory.
